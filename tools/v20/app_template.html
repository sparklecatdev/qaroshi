A four-part speaking routine

1. Segment. Treat sh, zh, kh and ng as single Qaroshi letters. 2. Stress. Place primary word stress on the penultimate syllable. 3. Connect. Keep suffixes attached, with breath groups at natural phrase boundaries. 4. Convey. Pronounce the written resonance word and use its social intent, without imposing a fixed pitch tune.

Quality, recording consent and source integrity

What is documented?

26 grapheme-to-phoneme values; six vowels; default penultimate stress; 16 pronounced written resonance markers; real canonical minimal contrasts; 4,383 controlled example sentences. IPA is broad phonemic citation pronunciation.

What is not documented?

Actual native speaker variation, dialect-specific vowels, mandatory intonation curves, acoustic durations, or native voice models. No recordings are bundled. Any user-generated audio requires separate consent and human review before publication.

Recording review process

1. Confirm consent. 2. Record the exact prompt. 3. Listen back. 4. Compare with IPA, intended stress, and the lexical meaning. 5. Check noise, clipping and whether the intended syllables were spoken. 6. Export audio and JSON only if the speaker approves.

Automatic loudness summaries are technical diagnostics only: they cannot judge pronunciation, dialect, or consent.

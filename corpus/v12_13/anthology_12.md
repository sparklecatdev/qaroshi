# Qaroshi 12: Original Editorial Anthology

Every passage is a separate narrative or essay, written for this edition and not sampled from spontaneous speech. All text retains mandatory written resonance; English is contextual, not speaker-validated.

## The Map That Wasn't a Map

*fictional narrative: A navigator learns that a precise tool cannot answer every social question.*

**sen mi rehëngo-m hari.**
I owned a compass.  
`/sen mi ɾe.ˈhə.ŋom ˈha.ɾi/`

**sen mi gëru-m tika-te.**
I had seen the mountain in the distance.  
`/sen mi ˈɡə.ɾum ti.ˈka.te/`

**sen mi wëru-m tika-te si.**
I had observed the forest myself.  
`/sen mi ˈwə.ɾum ti.ˈka.te si/`

**sen mi shima-m shëliro-te.**
I had also read a book about the route.  
`/sen mi ˈʃi.mam ʃə.li.ˈɾo.te/`

**zher mi sëma-m nu sëmi.**
Even so, I didn't know the whole truth.  
`/ʒeɾ mi ˈsə.mam nu ˈsə.mi/`

**hë ti mi-wa goro-yu he?**
Will you fly with me?  
`/hə ti ˈmi.wa ɡo.ˈɾo.ju he/`

**sen mi mara-m sharoa-te.**
I had asked the friend directly.  
`/sen mi ˈma.ɾam ʃa.ɾo.ˈa.te/`

**wen mi mara-m nashi.**
I cared for that friend across the distance.  
`/wen mi ˈma.ɾam ˈna.ʃi/`

**sen sa shiwaro-m shiwari-te.**
They sent a message.  
`/sen sa ʃi.ˈwa.ɾom ʃi.wa.ˈɾi.te/`

**sen mi shiwaro-m shëliro-te.**
I read it carefully.  
`/sen mi ʃi.ˈwa.ɾom ʃə.li.ˈɾo.te/`

**zher sa goro-ho nu hari.**
They did not have permission to fly.  
`/ʒeɾ sa ɡo.ˈɾo.ho nu ˈha.ɾi/`

**sen mi rehëngo-m tika-te.**
I looked at the compass again.  
`/sen mi ɾe.ˈhə.ŋom ti.ˈka.te/`

**sen mi shënoa-m hari.**
I had a choice.  
`/sen mi ʃə.ˈno.am ˈha.ɾi/`

**qor mi mara-m daru-yu.**
I would respect my friend rather than a timetable.  
`/qoɾ mi ˈma.ɾam da.ˈɾu.ju/`

**nëm mi naro-gu gura-te.**
I traveled home instead.  
`/nəm mi na.ˈɾo.ɡu ɡu.ˈɾa.te/`

**sen mi moru-m shiroli-te.**
That evening I recorded what had happened.  
`/sen mi ˈmo.ɾum ʃi.ɾo.ˈli.te/`

**sen mi rehëngo-m daru-te.**
I kept the compass safe.  
`/sen mi ɾe.ˈhə.ŋom da.ˈɾu.te/`

**nëm mi shëno-m hari.**
But I had one question left.  
`/nəm mi ˈʃə.nom ˈha.ɾi/`

## The Honest Report

*essay: Why source-of-evidence particles matter more than sounding confident.*

**sen mi shiwaro-m shëliro-te.**
I read a message.  
`/sen mi ʃi.ˈwa.ɾom ʃə.li.ˈɾo.te/`

**sen sa goro-te hu.**
The message said someone had flown.  
`/sen sa ɡo.ˈɾo.te hu/`

**zher mi sa-m nu tika-te.**
I had not seen that person.  
`/ʒeɾ mi sam nu ti.ˈka.te/`

**sen mi qaro-m nu tika-te.**
I had not seen the Nardoragon either.  
`/sen mi ˈqa.ɾom nu ti.ˈka.te/`

**sen mi shëno-m hari.**
That gave me a question.  
`/sen mi ˈʃə.nom ˈha.ɾi/`

**hë ti sa-m tika-te si he?**
Did you see that person yourself?  
`/hə ti sam ti.ˈka.te si he/`

**sen sa sa-m nu tika-te.**
They had not seen the person.  
`/sen sa sam nu ti.ˈka.te/`

**qor mi sëma-m daru-yu.**
I decided to protect the difference between report and truth.  
`/qoɾ mi ˈsə.mam da.ˈɾu.ju/`

**sen mi shiwaro-m shiroli-te.**
I wrote a second message.  
`/sen mi ʃi.ˈwa.ɾom ʃi.ɾo.ˈli.te/`

**sen mi moru-m nu hari.**
I had no first-hand memory of the event.  
`/sen mi ˈmo.ɾum nu ˈha.ɾi/`

**zher mi sa-m nu sëmi.**
I admitted I did not know what had happened.  
`/ʒeɾ mi sam nu ˈsə.mi/`

**sen mi mara-m sharoa-te.**
I asked a friend instead.  
`/sen mi ˈma.ɾam ʃa.ɾo.ˈa.te/`

**sen mi moru-m shiroli-te.**
I documented my own memory separately.  
`/sen mi ˈmo.ɾum ʃi.ɾo.ˈli.te/`

**nëm mi shërohi.**
The investigation was tiring.  
`/nəm mi ʃə.ˈɾo.hi/`

**sen mi naro-lo nëru-te.**
I rested at home.  
`/sen mi na.ˈɾo.lo nə.ˈɾu.te/`

**dal mi yurayaro shëno-m sharoa-yu.**
I would ask again the following day.  
`/dal mi ju.ɾa.ˈja.ɾo ˈʃə.nom ʃa.ɾo.ˈa.ju/`

## The Book Returned with Notes

*fictional narrative: A library incident becomes a lesson about ownership and shared learning.*

**sen mi shima-m hari.**
I had a book.  
`/sen mi ˈʃi.mam ˈha.ɾi/`

**sen mi shima-m shëliro-te.**
I read the book slowly.  
`/sen mi ˈʃi.mam ʃə.li.ˈɾo.te/`

**sen mi shëno-m hari.**
I encountered a question.  
`/sen mi ˈʃə.nom ˈha.ɾi/`

**sen mi moru-m shiroli-te.**
I wrote down a memory the page evoked.  
`/sen mi ˈmo.ɾum ʃi.ɾo.ˈli.te/`

**sen mi shima-m shiroli-te.**
Then I wrote in the book itself.  
`/sen mi ˈʃi.mam ʃi.ɾo.ˈli.te/`

**zher mi shima-m nu daru-te.**
I had failed to protect the borrowed volume.  
`/ʒeɾ mi ˈʃi.mam nu da.ˈɾu.te/`

**lën mi përë.**
I apologized to its keeper.  
`/lən mi ˈpə.ɾə/`

**sen mi shima-m pëli-te.**
I returned the book.  
`/sen mi ˈʃi.mam pə.ˈli.te/`

**hë ti mi-m shiroawe-yu he?**
Will you listen to my explanation?  
`/hə ti mim ʃi.ɾo.a.ˈwe.ju he/`

**sen sa mi-m shiroawe-te.**
They listened.  
`/sen sa mim ʃi.ɾo.a.ˈwe.te/`

**sen mi moru-m shiroli-yu.**
I promised to write my notes separately next time.  
`/sen mi ˈmo.ɾum ʃi.ɾo.ˈli.ju/`

**qor mi shima-m daru-yu.**
I would protect the shared copy.  
`/qoɾ mi ˈʃi.mam da.ˈɾu.ju/`

**sen sa mi-m yarëmi-te.**
The keeper taught me how to make an annotation.  
`/sen sa mim ja.ɾə.ˈmi.te/`

**rëm mi rëmi-te.**
I learned something more valuable than a rule.  
`/ɾəm mi ɾə.ˈmi.te/`

**nëm naru shima-m shëliro-yu.**
We planned to read together again.  
`/nəm ˈna.ɾu ˈʃi.mam ʃə.li.ˈɾo.ju/`

## Two Meanings of Home

*reflective essay: A building can offer shelter without automatically granting belonging.*

**sen mi naro-m tika-te.**
I saw a building that looked like home.  
`/sen mi ˈna.ɾom ti.ˈka.te/`

**sen mi raro-m tika-te.**
I noticed its door.  
`/sen mi ˈɾa.ɾom ti.ˈka.te/`

**sen mi tëdiba-m tika-te.**
I saw its window.  
`/sen mi tə.ˈdi.bam ti.ˈka.te/`

**sen mi naro-gu gura-te.**
I traveled toward it.  
`/sen mi na.ˈɾo.ɡu ɡu.ˈɾa.te/`

**hë mi naro-gu nëra-ho he?**
May I stay here?  
`/hə mi na.ˈɾo.ɡu nə.ˈɾa.ho he/`

**sen mi naro-lo nëra-te.**
I was allowed inside.  
`/sen mi na.ˈɾo.lo nə.ˈɾa.te/`

**nëm mi naro-lo nëru-te.**
I rested there quietly.  
`/nəm mi na.ˈɾo.lo nə.ˈɾu.te/`

**zher mi runa-m nu hari.**
I did not yet have a chosen family.  
`/ʒeɾ mi ˈɾu.nam nu ˈha.ɾi/`

**sen mi mara-m hari.**
But I did have a friend.  
`/sen mi ˈma.ɾam ˈha.ɾi/`

**më mi mara-m nashi.**
I cared about that friend.  
`/mə mi ˈma.ɾam ˈna.ʃi/`

**sen sa mi-tu kima-m pëli-te.**
They gave me water.  
`/sen sa ˈmi.tu ˈki.mam pə.ˈli.te/`

**sen mi kima-m këmë-te.**
I drank it.  
`/sen mi ˈki.mam kə.ˈmə.te/`

**sen mi shëna-m hari.**
Little by little, trust grew.  
`/sen mi ˈʃə.nam ˈha.ɾi/`

**sen mi runa-m hari.**
Eventually I could speak of chosen family.  
`/sen mi ˈɾu.nam ˈha.ɾi/`

**sen mi naro-m daru-yu.**
I wanted to care for the home too.  
`/sen mi ˈna.ɾom da.ˈɾu.ju/`

**qor mi shëna-m daru-yu.**
And to protect the trust that made it a home.  
`/qoɾ mi ˈʃə.nam da.ˈɾu.ju/`

## What a Promise Can Contain

*philosophical essay: Distinguishing a meaningful promise from an impossible guarantee.*

**hë ti namu-m hari he?**
Do you have a promise?  
`/hə ti ˈna.mum ˈha.ɾi he/`

**sen mi namu-m hari.**
I do.  
`/sen mi ˈna.mum ˈha.ɾi/`

**zher mi wukëro-m nu sëmi.**
But I cannot know the whole future.  
`/ʒeɾ mi wu.ˈkə.ɾom nu ˈsə.mi/`

**sen mi nënayaro-m tika.**
I can see what is here today.  
`/sen mi nə.na.ˈja.ɾom ˈti.ka/`

**sen mi mara-m nashi.**
I care for my friend.  
`/sen mi ˈma.ɾam ˈna.ʃi/`

**qor mi nënayaro ti-wa nëra-yu.**
I promise to stay with them today.  
`/qoɾ mi nə.na.ˈja.ɾo ˈti.wa nə.ˈɾa.ju/`

**zher mi sëna nu sëmi.**
I cannot certify forever.  
`/ʒeɾ mi ˈsə.na nu ˈsə.mi/`

**hë ti goro-ki he?**
Can you physically fly?  
`/hə ti ɡo.ˈɾo.ki he/`

**sen mi goro-ki.**
I can.  
`/sen mi ɡo.ˈɾo.ki/`

**hë ti goro-ho he?**
Are you permitted to?  
`/hə ti ɡo.ˈɾo.ho he/`

**sen mi goro-ho.**
Sometimes I am.  
`/sen mi ɡo.ˈɾo.ho/`

**sen mi goro-du.**
Sometimes duty requires flight.  
`/sen mi ɡo.ˈɾo.du/`

**sen mi goro-yu.**
A future flight is another question again.  
`/sen mi ɡo.ˈɾo.ju/`

**sen mi namu-m shiroli-yu.**
I will write the promise down.  
`/sen mi ˈna.mum ʃi.ɾo.ˈli.ju/`

**sen mi shiwaro-m shiwari-yu.**
And send it clearly.  
`/sen mi ʃi.ˈwa.ɾom ʃi.wa.ˈɾi.ju/`

**qor mi mara-m daru-yu.**
The commitment will be concrete rather than grandiose.  
`/qoɾ mi ˈma.ɾam da.ˈɾu.ju/`

## The Night Watch

*fictional report: A watcher must decide when to send a storm warning.*

**sen mi yami-lo nëra-te.**
I was on watch during the night.  
`/sen mi ja.ˈmi.lo nə.ˈɾa.te/`

**sen mi wena-m tika-te.**

`/sen mi ˈwe.nam ti.ˈka.te/`

`/sen mi ki.ˈma.zum ti.ˈka.te si/`

**khar mi lushënga-m tika-te.**

`/xaɾ mi lu.ˈʃə.ŋam ti.ˈka.te/`

**sen mi shiwaro-m shiroli-te.**

`/sen mi ʃi.ˈwa.ɾom ʃi.ɾo.ˈli.te/`

Will you send the warning?  
`/hə ti ʃi.ˈwa.ɾom ʃi.wa.ˈɾi.ju he/`

I would.  
`/qoɾ mi ʃi.ˈwa.ɾom ʃi.wa.ˈɾi.ju/`

`/sen mi ˈma.ɾam ʃa.ɾo.ˈa.te/`

`/sen sa ˈɡə.ɾum ti.ˈka.te si/`

`/qoɾ ˈna.ɾu ˈna.ɾom da.ˈɾu.ju/`

`/nəm ˈna.ɾu na.ˈɾo.lo nə.ˈɾu.ju/`

## The Question in a Painted Wing

*fictional arts dialogue retold as prose: An image raises a memory its maker did not intend.*

**sen mi mërawë-m shiroli-te.**
I painted a wing.  
`/sen mi mə.ˈɾa.wəm ʃi.ɾo.ˈli.te/`

**sen mi banu-m tika-te.**
I had studied a real wing.  
`/sen mi ˈba.num ti.ˈka.te/`

**sen mi mëru-m tika-te.**
I had looked closely at a feather.  
`/sen mi ˈmə.ɾum ti.ˈka.te/`

**sen mi mërawë-m daru-te.**
I cared for the painting.  
`/sen mi mə.ˈɾa.wəm da.ˈɾu.te/`

**sen mi mara-m sharoa-te.**
I asked a friend to see it.  
`/sen mi ˈma.ɾam ʃa.ɾo.ˈa.te/`

**sen sa mërawë-m tika-te.**
They looked at the work.  
`/sen sa mə.ˈɾa.wəm ti.ˈka.te/`

`/suɾ sa ˈmo.ɾum ˈha.ɾi/`

`/sen mi na.ˈɾo.lo nə.ˈɾa.te/`

`/nəm mi mo.ɾu.ˈʃi.ɾam ʃi.ɾo.ˈli.te/`

`/qoɾ mi ˈma.ɾam da.ˈɾu.ju/`

`/sen mi qa.ˈɾo.ʃim ˈɾə.mi/`

`/nəm mi ˈʃi.mam ʃə.li.ˈɾo.ju/`

## A Letter Sent Twice

*epistolary fiction: A message is sent, but a better reply requires listening first.*

**sen mi ti-tu shiwaro-m shiwari-te.**

`/sen mi ˈti.tu ʃi.ˈwa.ɾom ʃi.wa.ˈɾi.te/`

`/sen mi ʃi.ˈwa.ɾom ʃi.wa.ˈɾi.te si/`

**zher mi ti-m nu ronaro-te.**

`/ʒeɾ mi tim nu ɾo.na.ˈɾo.te/`

`/sen mi ˈmo.ɾum nu ˈha.ɾi/`

`/sen mi ˈma.ɾam ʃa.ɾo.ˈa.te/`

They had no reply either.  
`/sen sa mim nu pə.ɾo.ˈa.te/`

**sen mi shiwaro-m shëliro-te.**
I reread what I had written.

**lën mi përë.**

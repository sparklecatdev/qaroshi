# Qaroshi 16.0 · Semantic Dictionary & Corpus Quality Handbook

> **Editorial development reference.** This handbook does not create approved senses, establish fixed idioms, or claim independent speaker or dialect evidence. It preserves the inherited 30,000-entry canonical dictionary and its grammar.

## 1. Evidence summary

- **30,000** existing canonical entries, including **13,379** transparent compounds.

- **143** different headwords appear in separately drafted editorial writing; **3,752** appear in controlled bilingual reference examples. These forms are not speaker-attested.

- **5,000** draft sense cards and **1,000** proposed figurative expressions require independent review.

- **57** new individually written lexical clarifications complement **61** previously written profiles.

- The 15.1 procedural 200,000+ token extension remains useful for drills, not as proof of naturalistic language use.

### Data-source hierarchy

A canonical headword establishes a published orthographic/lexical entry. A controlled example demonstrates a draft grammar construction. An independently scripted editorial dialogue offers a constructed context. A procedural template is suitable for software coverage. **None alone establishes a community convention or field attestation.**

### Specific audit findings

- **4,388 / 5,000** older sense cards have no usage illustration in either the controlled parallel reference or the separately drafted corpus. Their source *definitions* are not thereby disproven.

- **1,000 / 1,000** idiom candidates have zero exact occurrences in the separately drafted editorial corpus. This does not prove they cannot become idioms.

- **223** groups of identical Qaroshi source sentences have varying contextual English renderings. Contextual paraphrase is not automatically a contradiction.

## 1a. Word inference and root transparency

The corpus can be learned through productive compounds, but not by predicting the meaning of arbitrary syllables. All 13,379 marked compounds have recorded modifier–head structure, recursively drawing from 1,820 base headwords. Knowing these bases enables *literal* decomposition, not automatic mastery of all technical senses. There are also 16,525 entries labeled headword without an equivalent compound structure in the dictionary, plus 96 function words. A word learner should not be expected to memorize 30,000 unrelated flashcards.

See **`Qaroshi_16_Roots_and_Inference.md`**, **`data/v16/productive_root_learning_ladder.csv`**, and the **Root Explorer & word builder** in the offline Semantic Atlas. These report computational coverage and preserve the original spellings; they do not invent etymologies.

## 2. Editorial meaning profiles

Each profile states a plausible narrower reading consistent with the inherited lexicon and warns where inference should stop. It is **not** automatically ratified.

### mara · friend; trusted peer

**Editorial interpretation:** An individual in a trusted peer relationship.

**Usage restriction:** Do not use for any stranger or automatically for kin.

**Contrast:** runa is a chosen family/group, not one friend.

**Example (editorial):** `më mi mara-m nashi.` · I cherish my friend.

### runa · chosen family; close-knit community

**Editorial interpretation:** A close community someone considers chosen family.

**Usage restriction:** A relationship designation, not automatically a shared building or biological family.

**Contrast:** naro is the physical home or nesting place.

**Example (editorial):** `sen mi runa-m hari.` · I have a chosen family.

### naro · home; nesting place; safe shelter

**Editorial interpretation:** A dwelling or protective home.

**Usage restriction:** May suggest emotional security, but does not inherently encode a specific person's consent.

**Contrast:** runa is belonging to chosen family.

**Example (editorial):** `nëm mi naro-lo nëru.` · I rest at home.

### banu · wing

**Editorial interpretation:** A literal wing or appendage used in flight.

**Usage restriction:** Metaphorical use requires documented context; not all wing references are idioms.

**Contrast:** goro denotes flight as an action.

**Example (editorial):** `sen mi banu-m tika.` · I see a wing.

### mëru · feather; soft plume

**Editorial interpretation:** A feather or soft plume.

**Usage restriction:** A detachable feather is not identical to the whole wing.

**Contrast:** banu is a wing.

**Example (editorial):** `sen mi mëru-m hari.` · I have a feather.

### goro · fly; move through the air

**Editorial interpretation:** To fly or move through the air.

**Usage restriction:** An unmarked occurrence says nothing about permission or obligation.

**Contrast:** goro-ki encodes ability; goro-ho permission.

**Example (editorial):** `sen mi goro-ki.` · I can fly.

### gura · go; travel away

**Editorial interpretation:** To go or travel away.

**Usage restriction:** Can take an overt destination with -gu; does not necessarily mean airborne travel.

**Contrast:** goro means to fly.

**Example (editorial):** `sen mi naro-gu gura-yu.` · I will travel home.

### kima · water

**Editorial interpretation:** Water as a liquid substance.

**Usage restriction:** Mass noun; do not automatically pluralize as discrete bottles.

**Contrast:** këmë is the action of drinking.

**Example (editorial):** `sen mi kima-m këmë.` · I drink water.

### këmë · drink; consume liquid

**Editorial interpretation:** To drink a liquid.

**Usage restriction:** The direct object names liquid consumed; do not equate drinking with thirst.

**Contrast:** kima is water, not the action.

**Example (editorial):** `hë ti kima-m këmë-yu he?` · Will you drink water?

### mërawë · painting; artistic image

**Editorial interpretation:** A painting or artistic image.

**Usage restriction:** A visual artwork; a story about it is a different noun.

**Contrast:** morushira is narrative content.

**Example (editorial):** `sen mi mërawë-m tika.` · I see a painting.

### shiwaro · message; spoken or written communication

**Editorial interpretation:** A spoken or written message.

**Usage restriction:** A message is not automatically read, agreed to, or even received.

**Contrast:** shiwari is sending; shëliro is reading.

**Example (editorial):** `sen mi shiwaro-m shiroli.` · I write a message.

### shiwari · send; transmit a message or item

**Editorial interpretation:** To transmit or send a message or item.

**Usage restriction:** Requires source/recipient context where relevant; sending is not reading.

**Contrast:** shëliro is reading, not delivery.

**Example (editorial):** `sen mi shiwaro-m shiwari-yu.` · I will send a message.

### ronaro · hear; perceive sound

**Editorial interpretation:** To hear a sound.

**Usage restriction:** Auditory detection does not imply full attention or comprehension.

**Contrast:** shiroawe is attentive listening.

**Example (editorial):** `hë ti mi-m ronaro he?` · Do you hear me?

### shiroawe · listen; attend to sound

**Editorial interpretation:** To listen or attend to sound.

**Usage restriction:** Do not equate merely detecting speech with attentive listening.

**Contrast:** ronaro concerns hearing.

**Example (editorial):** `sen mi ti-m shiroawe-yu.` · I will listen to you.

### sëma · truth; accurate information

**Editorial interpretation:** Truth or accurate information.

**Usage restriction:** A proposition's truth is distinct from evidence type or speaker certainty.

**Contrast:** sëmi means possessing knowledge.

**Example (editorial):** `qor naru sëma-m daru-yu.` · We will protect the truth.

### sëmi · know; possess reliable knowledge

**Editorial interpretation:** To know or possess reliable knowledge.

**Usage restriction:** Direct observation, hearsay and inference are source markers, not automatic knowledge.

**Contrast:** shërawë is belief/acceptance as true.

**Example (editorial):** `sen mi sëma-m sëmi.` · I know the truth.

### shërawë · believe; accept as true

**Editorial interpretation:** To believe or accept as true.

**Usage restriction:** Belief alone does not guarantee truth or first-hand observation.

**Contrast:** sëmi implies reliable knowledge.

**Example (editorial):** `zher mi sëma-m nu sëmi.` · I do not know the truth.

### shëna · trust; confidence in someone

**Editorial interpretation:** Trust or confidence in another.

**Usage restriction:** Abstract state; seeing a trust is generally a figurative/metalinguistic construction.

**Contrast:** namu is a promise, which does not guarantee trust.

**Example (editorial):** `sen mi shëna-m hari.` · I have trust.

### nashi · love deeply; cherish; care for

**Editorial interpretation:** To cherish or care deeply for another.

**Usage restriction:** Not automatically sexual, romantic, reciprocal or permanent.

**Contrast:** rawëli is desire/want, not deep care.

**Example (editorial):** `më mi ti-m nashi.` · I cherish you.

### shënalo · smile; show happiness

**Editorial interpretation:** To smile or show happiness.

**Usage restriction:** A smile does not entail consent or agreement.

**Contrast:** këmuro means to agree.

**Example (editorial):** `zha mi shënalo.` · I smile joyfully.

### daru · protect; guard

**Editorial interpretation:** To protect or guard.

**Usage restriction:** May take a person or object; claiming protection does not establish actual safety.

**Contrast:** hari is possession, not protection.

**Example (editorial):** `qor mi ti-m daru-yu.` · I will protect you.

### qaro · Nardoragon; member of the species

**Editorial interpretation:** A member of the Nardoragon species.

**Usage restriction:** A species noun, not a grammatical gender or personality judgement.

**Contrast:** mara is a social relationship.

**Example (editorial):** `sen mi qaro-m tika.` · I see a Nardoragon.

### shira · song; sung composition

**Editorial interpretation:** A song or musical composition.

**Usage restriction:** The object or work is distinct from the speech/song performance.

**Contrast:** shiri means speak, not universally sing.

**Example (editorial):** `sen mi shira-m shëliro.` · I read a song.

### shiri · speak; say words

**Editorial interpretation:** To speak or say words.

**Usage restriction:** Does not guarantee the listener heard or understood.

**Contrast:** ronaro means hear and shiroawe means listen.

**Example (editorial):** `sen mi ti-wa shiri-yu.` · I will speak with you.

### shiroli · write; mark language on a surface

**Editorial interpretation:** To write or mark language on a surface.

**Usage restriction:** Writing a message does not mean it was sent or received.

**Example (editorial):** `sen mi moru-m shiroli-yu.` · I will write a memory.

### pëli · give; offer

**Editorial interpretation:** To give or offer.

**Contrast:** pëmi is to buy.

**Example (editorial):** `më mi ti-tu kima-m pëli-yu.` · I will offer you water.

### haro · good; safe; well

**Editorial interpretation:** Good, safe, or well in context.

**Usage restriction:** English 'good', 'safe', 'well' overlap only when context licenses; not universally interchangeable.

**Contrast:** shëmoa specifically means broken/not functioning.

**Example (editorial):** `hë ti haro he?` · Are you well?

### shërohi · tired; fatigued

**Editorial interpretation:** Tired or fatigued.

**Usage restriction:** Not a synonym for sickness or emotional grief; may coexist.

**Contrast:** sura denotes grief.

**Example (editorial):** `sur mi shërohi.` · I am tired.

### lësha · cute; endearing

**Editorial interpretation:** Cute or endearing.

**Usage restriction:** Affectionate evaluation, not an automatic assertion of consent or romantic interest.

**Contrast:** nashi means cherish.

**Example (editorial):** `më ti lësha.` · You are cute.

### shëmoa · broken; not functioning

**Editorial interpretation:** Broken or not functioning.

**Usage restriction:** A device can be broken without a person being unwell; do not use as a generic negative.

**Contrast:** haro has positive contextual senses.

**Example (editorial):** `rak shenaro shëmoa.` · The device is broken!

### përoa · answer; reply

**Editorial interpretation:** To answer or reply.

**Usage restriction:** A reply can be inaccurate and need not resolve the original question.

**Example (editorial):** `sen mi ti-m përoa-yu.` · I will reply to you.

### përë · apologize; say sorry

**Example (editorial):** `lën mi përë.` · I apologize.

## 6. Review and publication procedure

1. Read the source dictionary entry, POS, canonical first gloss and compound components.
2. Inspect editorial and controlled usage independently; do not treat procedural frequency as naturalness.
3. Compare competing meanings against full context, participant roles, clause scopes and evidential markings.
4. Add **negative examples** showing when a tempting English paraphrase is too broad.
5. For a new sense, distinguish an English paraphrase of one meaning from a genuinely distinct lexical meaning.
6. For an idiom, provide clear, externally accepted conventional usage. An untested metaphor remains a proposal.
7. Record source, independent reviewer, date, decision, corrected translation, and evidence; reject or revise claims without erasing history.
8. Only a separately versioned standardization decision can update the canonical lexicon.

# Qaroshi dictionary conventions

All 16,732 entries appear in one unified dictionary, with stable identifiers and both English→Qaroshi and Qaroshi→English lookup. Definitions are semantic headwords, not a guarantee that every possible metaphorical or dialect meaning has been listed. Every term includes a word class and semantic domain.

## Headwords, derivation, and inflection

A **headword** is an independent lexical item. A **compound** combines two lexical items in modifier–head order and may become an established specialized term. A **grammatical form** changes tense, aspect, number, case or modality using the grammar rules and is generally *not counted* as a separate dictionary entry.

For example, `qaro` is a headword, while `qaroshi` has an interpretable compound analysis `qaro + shi` and specialized cultural meaning. The specific root components of a 5.0 transparent compound are supplied in its dictionary entry. The final component is the semantic head; the first narrows the intended meaning. English glosses for highly specialized compounds may require context, and terms with similar English glosses are not automatically perfect synonyms.

Components are clickable on the wiki page when they themselves have entries. A component link describes the underlying lexical element, not necessarily every historical or idiomatic use.

## Phonetics and writing

The Romanization uses 26 native graphemes, including `ë`, `sh`, `zh`, `kh` and `ng`. Each has a distinct handwritten glyph. Each dictionary entry includes broad phonemic IPA, a penultimate-stress reading guide and an English-style approximation. Only IPA encodes Qaroshi-specific phonetic contrasts reliably: `q`, `kh`, and tapped `r` have no exact English equivalents.

## How meaning is indexed

Entries are searchable by Qaroshi form, English gloss, domain, phonetic approximation and IPA. Search tolerates the omission of diacritics in the query, so `gero` can find `gëro`; the official spelling remains `gëro`.

The English index includes multiple gloss senses separated by semicolons. Inflectional variants are generated by grammar and need not be listed as additional headwords.

## Contribution rules

New lexical items should have a distinct written form, clearly differentiated meaning, POS, domain, and complete pronunciation. New compounds should be semantically compositional or have an explicitly explained specialized sense. Avoid padding the dictionary with inflections, repeating near-identical glossary phrases, or assigning roots to unrelated meanings without a documented extension.

For editorial changes, preserve all existing stable IDs and update `data/lexicon.csv`, `data/lexicon.json`, the English index and compiled wiki together; then run all tests. The old canon digest must remain stable unless a deliberate, documented normative correction is made.

The teaching phrasebook is controlled pedagogical content, not a measure of spontaneous speaker usage.

The bilingual teaching corpus is a collection of controlled examples for grammar practice. It is not presented as spontaneous spoken-language attestation.

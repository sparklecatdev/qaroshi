# Qaroshi dictionary conventions

All 30,000 canonical entries appear in one unified dictionary, with stable identifiers and both English→Qaroshi and Qaroshi→English lookup. Definitions are semantic headwords, not a guarantee that every possible metaphorical or dialect meaning has been listed. Every term includes a word class and semantic domain.

## Headwords, derivation, and inflection

A **headword** is an independent lexical item. A **compound** combines two lexical items in modifier–head order and may become an established specialized term. A **grammatical form** changes tense, aspect, number, case or modality using the grammar rules and is generally *not counted* as a separate dictionary entry.

For example, `qaro` is a headword, while `qaroshi` has an interpretable compound analysis `qaro + shi` and specialized cultural meaning. The specific root components of a 5.0 transparent compound are supplied in its dictionary entry. The final component is the semantic head; the first narrows the intended meaning. English glosses for highly specialized compounds may require context, and terms with similar English glosses are not automatically perfect synonyms.

Components are clickable on the wiki page when they themselves have entries. A component link describes the underlying lexical element, not necessarily every historical or idiomatic use.

## Phonetics and writing

The Romanization uses 26 native graphemes, including `ë`, `sh`, `zh`, `kh` and `ng`. Each has a distinct handwritten glyph. Each dictionary entry includes broad phonemic IPA, a penultimate-stress reading guide and an English-style approximation. Only IPA encodes Qaroshi-specific phonetic contrasts reliably: `q`, `kh`, and tapped `r` have no exact English equivalents.

## How meaning is indexed

Entries are searchable by Qaroshi form, English gloss, domain, phonetic approximation and IPA. Search tolerates the omission of diacritics in the query, so `gero` can find `gëro`; the official spelling remains `gëro`.

The English index includes multiple gloss senses separated by semicolons. Inflectional variants are generated by grammar and need not be listed as additional headwords.

## Lexical repair and word senses · 24–27 amendments

A canonical headword is a word, not an English replacement macro. In the revision 24–27, **22 additional word-sense specifications** clarify existing headwords without renaming them. For example, `nashi` deep cherishing does not imply `mali` romance; `sëmi` knowledge cannot be derived merely from the event of `tika` seeing something; `noa` is not an automatic translation of low bandwidth or low vision. These are **semantic exclusions** that affect when existing words may be used, not an increase in the 30,000-headword count.

To decide whether an opaque word should be replaced, compare its lexical history and the proposed compound's literal **component senses**. The v16.2 corpus contains 44 editorial trials, 9 holds, and 12 rejected false-friend constructions, plus unreviewed machine-ranked candidates. **None of those proposals becomes canonical by virtue of this amendment.** A stable existing spelling is preserved until a separate human-reviewed migration adopts a replacement, documents synonyms/collisions, updates the bilingual corpus and references, and provides an old→new alias. Otherwise the learner is forced to memorize two conflicting “official” spellings.

The case `-za` is **productive grammar**, not a new dictionary root. It means an overt actor **of a passive predicate**, distinct from instrumental `-shi`. Thus `mara-za` is a compositional case form, not an additional headword.

### Predictable compounds and collision safety

1. Choose an existing modifier root and an existing head with the **correct sense**, not merely a matching English word.
2. The head determines the compound's main semantic category. A compound may be narrower than its literal gloss, but this narrowing must be explained in an accepted sense record.
3. A transparent description is not automatically a conventional dictionary word. If two interpretations collide, do not promote the proposed form.
4. Preserve official component spelling, the six vowel qualities and the original 26 graphemes. Compound joins do not permit deleting an inherited vowel merely to make a form shorter.
5. Examine whether the proposal requires a false-friend sense (e.g., gratuity *tip* versus animal-tail tip). An attractive English decomposition alone is not a passing review.

For the focused grammatical and semantic amendments see [24–27 Linguistic Reform](language_reform_24_27.md). The source-aligned word senses are in `data/semantics/curated_senses.json`.

## Contribution rules

New lexical items should have a distinct written form, clearly differentiated meaning, POS, domain, and complete pronunciation. New compounds should be semantically compositional or have an explicitly explained specialized sense. Avoid padding the dictionary with inflections, repeating near-identical glossary phrases, or assigning roots to unrelated meanings without a documented extension.

For editorial changes, preserve all existing stable IDs and update `data/lexicon.csv`, `data/lexicon.json`, the English index and compiled wiki together; then run all tests. The old canon digest must remain stable unless a deliberate, documented normative correction is made.

The teaching phrasebook is controlled pedagogical content, not a measure of spontaneous speaker usage.

The bilingual teaching corpus is a collection of controlled examples for grammar practice. It is not presented as spontaneous spoken-language attestation.

## Reading entries as a lexicographer

A headword is a spelling connected to a defined lexical sense and grammatical behavior. It is not an instruction to substitute the English gloss wherever an English writer happens to use the same letters. The English gloss *safe* might represent a state, a safety-related device or an instruction to avoid danger; these belong to different semantic categories even if English shares the word. The lexicon therefore separates **part of speech**, **definition/sense**, **domain**, **word components**, **usage note** and **phonetics**.

### Three tests before choosing a word

**Semantic test:** What is the exact intended meaning? If an English term is polysemous, locate the correct sense. **Syntactic test:** Is the entry a noun, verb, adjective, adverb or function word? Do not force a noun directly into a verb slot without a documented conversion process. **Pragmatic test:** Does the entry imply romance, family, ownership, legal status, permission, or a technical property that the source actually intends? Avoid silently intensifying the claim.

## Inflected forms are predictable, but lexical senses are not

`qaro-ri` is the regular plural of `qaro`, not a second unrelated entry with a new root. `goro-te-se` expresses past continuous flying by ordinary verb morphology. By contrast, `qaroshi` is a specific lexical compound, because the conventional meaning “the Nardoragon language” matters. When a reader sees `naro`, `naro-gu` and `naro-de`, the differences are grammatical roles; when a reader sees a head-final compound, the definition may be specialized beyond the simplest literal sum.

Compounding works modifier-first and head-last in documented examples. That does not mean any two dictionary words can be concatenated and accepted automatically. A new technical term needs unambiguous root choices, a well-documented semantic relationship and a natural use in a sentence. Where the relationship cannot be expressed cleanly by a compound, a content clause or relative clause may be clearer.

## Using the reverse index critically

English-to-Qaroshi lookup is best treated as a list of possible senses. It does not demonstrate that two results are mutually interchangeable; they may belong to different domains or word classes. Exact Qaroshi spelling has priority over accent-folded search. In particular, `ë` and `e` are distinct native vowels even though the search tool may allow `gero` to retrieve `gëro`. The displayed dictionary entry, not the approximate search string, is the spelling to use in writing.

## Stable identifiers and maintenance

A lexical entry has a stable `id`, enabling citations across the wiki and data exports. A word page may have related topical entries, but those are **not a formal synonym set**. For careful cross-referencing, cite the exact headword and ID, record which definition was used, and retain the original broad IPA fields. If a definition is clarified later, distinguish an editorial gloss improvement from a change in the word's denotation. The [Dictionary mastery guide](deep_guides/dictionary_compounding.md) expands this into a full practical workflow.

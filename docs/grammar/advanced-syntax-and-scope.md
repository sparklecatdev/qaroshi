# Qaroshi: advanced grammar and scope

This reference addendum documents clear distinctions for complex translation and structural validation. It does **not** claim that all natural-language meanings can be automatically parsed.

## Mandatory written resonance

Every complete independent Qaroshi sentence begins with exactly one of these typed markers: `më, zha, qor, sen, hë, lën, sur, rak, khar, yë, nëm, wen, dal, rëm, hol, zher`. Subordinate clauses inherit the sentence's resonance. A separately quoted complete sentence may carry its own independent resonance, belonging to the quoted speaker.

`hë ti haro he?` asks “Are you well?” as a genuine inquiry. `khar ti haro he?` poses the same literal question with alarm. Resonance expresses the intended interpersonal stance; it is not an evidential or tense.

## Clause order

The neutral finite clause is **SUBJECT – TIME – OBJECT – OBLIGUES – [nu] – PREDICATE – EVIDENTIAL – SPEECH ACT**, preceded by the mandatory opening resonance at sentence level. Qaroshi is normally SOV. Direct objects use `-m`, recipients `-tu`, locations `-lo`, destinations `-gu`, origins `-de`, companions `-wa`, and instruments/passive agents `-shi`.

`sen mi kima-m këmë.` = “I drink water.”

## Nominal and verbal affix order

Nominal suffix slots follow **root – derivation – endearment – plural – case – topic**. The case slot admits only one case: `mara-ri-m` (plural direct object) is structurally well-ordered, while `mara-m-ri` is not. Distinct case relations require separate phrases, rather than two case suffixes on one noun.

Verbal suffix slots follow **root – voice/valency – tense – aspect – modality – tender manner**. Voice may be causative `-ru`, passive `-li`, reciprocal `-wo`, or reflexive `-ne`. Tense is past `-te` or future `-yu`; aspect is progressive `-se`, completive `-mo`, or habitual `-në`. Modal endings are mutually exclusive: ability `-ki`, permission `-ho`, obligation `-du`.

`sen sa goro-ki.` means “They can fly” (ability), whereas `sen sa goro-ho.` expresses authorization. Conflating ability with permission changes the proposition.

## Negation, quotation and evidence

The negator `nu` directly precedes the predicate it negates. `sen mi nu shiri.` = “I do not speak.” Negating ability (`nu shiri-ki`) is not automatically “I am able to choose not to speak.” Scope must be explicitly differentiated when stakes are high.

An independent yes/no question ends with `he`; a command/request ends with `ya`. These are not stacked as ordinary sentence endings. Evidence particles such as `si` (witnessed), `hu` (reported), and `ye` (inferred) describe how the speaker knows a claim. They do not encode whether a claim is true, nor whether a speaker likes it.

## Nested propositions and ambiguity

`wë` introduces a condition, `ro` a reason, `ko` a purpose, `uro` a concession, and `do` a reported proposition. In long complex sentences, comma and quotation boundaries should be visible in learner and technical Romanization. Reference ambiguity should be resolved by repeating the relevant noun rather than relying on a third-person pronoun with multiple possible antecedents.

A structural checker can detect a missing resonance, suffix ordering errors and conflicts between `he` and `ya`. It cannot by itself certify that a translation is idiomatic, historically attested, semantically true or fully unambiguous. Preserve unresolved readings and seek human review.

## Integrity policy

Do not alter the original 26 native glyph designs or the original 16 written resonance markers without explicit standards revision. Language additions should include part of speech, definition, grammatical frame, IPA, English rendering, example with resonance, and compatibility tests.

# Qaroshi practice and editing workbook

This workbook is meant to be used **after** the 16-lesson course. It includes 10 error diagnoses, a grammar-to-English checklist, and a 36-sentence mixed assessment drawn from the established phrasebook. Do not use the mistakes below as valid speech models.

## The editor’s ten traps

For each deliberately flawed line, identify the error and explain why the suggested correction is more accurate. Some examples are schematic, and the critique explicitly notes when context determines the right revision.

### Trap 1
**Check this line:** `mi ti-m nashi.`
**Diagnosis:** Missing resonance at the beginning; use the register appropriate to intention; `më mi ti-m nashi.` models affection.

### Trap 2
**Check this line:** `hë ti haro.`
**Diagnosis:** If a direct yes/no question is intended, add terminal `he`: `hë ti haro he?`. Otherwise the utterance is an incomplete question model.

### Trap 3
**Check this line:** `sen mi -te goro.`
**Diagnosis:** The tense suffix attaches after the verb root, not as a separate word: `sen mi goro-te.`

### Trap 4
**Check this line:** `sen mi qaro-gu gura.`
**Diagnosis:** This denotes travel toward a Nardoragon. If the intent is travel *from* them, the case is `-de`; do not confuse the two.

### Trap 5
**Check this line:** `më mi ti-m nashi he ya.`
**Diagnosis:** A question ending and a request ending do not coexist on the same clause. Choose the intended speech act.

### Trap 6
**Check this line:** `sen mi nu-te goro.`
**Diagnosis:** Negation `nu` is a separate particle immediately before the entire inflected predicate, not an internal verbal suffix.

### Trap 7
**Check this line:** `sen qaro-ri goro-te hu si.`
**Diagnosis:** Do not stack competing basic source-of-evidence endings as though they were conjugations; clarify whether the information is witnessed or reported.

### Trap 8
**Check this line:** `më mi ti-m nashi. ti haro.`
**Diagnosis:** The second full sentence needs a new resonance: e.g. `më ti haro.` when it too is affectionate.

### Trap 9
**Check this line:** `sen mi mara-lo kima-m pëli.`
**Diagnosis:** If the friend receives water, use dative `-tu`, not locative `-lo`: `sen mi mara-tu kima-m pëli.`

### Trap 10
**Check this line:** `më pëla ti nu zëru he?`
**Diagnosis:** A request uses `ya`; a genuine yes/no question uses `he`. Do not treat punctuation as a substitute.

## Rubric for self-evaluation

A fully accurate answer preserves participants, direction and possession; event time/aspect; ability versus permission versus obligation; negation and quantifier scope; sentence-initial resonance; speech-act ending; and evidence attribution. Natural English wording can vary while these grammatical distinctions remain constant. Partial credit is appropriate for correct semantic content when the emotional frame is omitted, but that omission should still be noticed in the analysis.

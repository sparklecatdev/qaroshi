# Qaroshi everyday conversation, repair, and social meaning

The 16 written resonance markers are obligatory on complete typed sentences. They encode intended emotional and interpersonal stance, not a uniform physical pitch. A standalone interjection may be an incomplete turn fragment, but a fully formed statement still needs a resonance.

## One proposition, five stances

- `hë ti haro he?`: Are you well? asked with curiosity.
- `khar ti haro he?`: Are you all right?! expressed with alarm.
- `zher ti haro he?`: Are you really okay? asked with uncertainty.
- `më ti haro he?`: Are you okay? asked tenderly.
- `sen ti haro he?`: Are you well? asked matter-of-factly.

## Fixing misunderstandings

Identify the part that was misunderstood, then replace it with a complete, marked sentence. The repair need not rely on English filler words: `sen mi kima-m nu këmë-te.` (I did not drink water), followed by `sen mi kima-m këmë-yu.` (I will drink water) explicitly corrects the tense. In dialogue with multiple participants, repeat the agent instead of relying on ambiguous `sa`.

## Refusal and autonomy

Ability `-ki` does not grant permission `-ho`; permission does not imply obligation `-du`. Polite `pëla` can soften a request. Optional `lan` can frame a request as freely declined where established. A person's refusal is valid regardless of the speaker's romantic language or warm resonance.

## Informal delivery

Speech may be quicker than writing: identical adjacent vowels can coalesce, and syllables can be reduced. The written grammar retains the full spelling, especially `ë`, the four digraph letters, and the initial resonance. Hesitation and interruptions belong in annotated transcripts; they should not become undocumented dictionary headwords.

## Testing naturalness

A coherent dialogue tracks speakers, background knowledge, current goals, whether a statement was directly witnessed or reported, the meaning of a refusal, and what each utterance changes in shared knowledge. A corpus generated by varying resonance markers is useful for drills but is not evidence of 1,000 independently spontaneous conversations.

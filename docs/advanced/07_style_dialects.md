# Style, register, dialects, multilinguality and language change

The **16 mandatory typed resonances** are not dialects. A resonance is a sentence-level social signal. A dialect is a systematic variety of a language distinguished by pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar or usage shared by a community. The reference has **one normative written variety**; no undocumented “coastal,” “mountain,” or “ancient” dialect should be presented as historically attested.

## 1. Three axes of variation

1. **Resonance**: the mandatory marker, e.g. `sen` neutral versus `më` warm. This is grammatically encoded.
2. **Style or register**: casual, formal, clinical, technical, poetic, vulgar, etc. This affects vocabulary and often the amount of ellipsis or explicit explanation.
3. **Dialect or idiolect**: systematic variation associated with a place, social network or individual speaker. This needs evidence rather than fictionalized historical claims.

Confusing these axes creates bad translations. An affectionate `më` sentence can contain a technical term; a formal legal sentence can be emotionally serious in `qor` without having an alternate legal grammar.

## 2. Formal and informal writing

Formal writing favors full subjects and explicit reference, constrained compound senses, source attribution, unambiguous scope and spelled-out requests. Informal chat can omit recoverable subjects and use context-dependent pronouns. Nevertheless, both standard styles retain one resonance marker per complete sentence. Emojis, capitalization and punctuation may coexist with it without taking its grammatical place.

## 3. Clinical and vulgar vocabulary

Select terms according to audience and purpose. Medical descriptions use clinical anatomical vocabulary; intimate conversation may use softer expressions or direct language based on the participants' preferences; profanity varies in force from interjection to personal abuse. A “strong expletive” should have a note describing whether it insults a person or simply expresses frustration. This is a semantic/register distinction, not a grammar loophole.

## 4. Code-switching and borrowing

A Nardoragon may quote English or a proper name in a Qaroshi sentence. In mixed-script digital text, **mark the boundary** of the foreign expression so the native 26-glyph converter does not misinterpret foreign `c`, `f`, `j`, `v` or `x`. Loanword accommodation is an explicit lexical process, not automatic transliteration by similar-looking letters. A proper name may remain in Latin characters when an agreed Qaroshi reading does not exist.

## 5. Creative innovation and standardization

The modifier–head compound pattern is productive, but some syntactically possible compounds are obscure. A speaker may coin one for poetry or a newly encountered device; a technical committee may later adopt a consistent head term. That does not mean an earlier convention was wrong. The dictionary keeps stable IDs and can add aliases, alternate senses or usage notes without silently redefining old words.

## 6. Dialect documentation template

For a future attested variety, collect examples with `variety_id`, `speaker_consent`, `source_date`, `phonetic_transcription`, `standard_correspondence`, `semantic_context`, `sample_size` and `confidence`. One isolated pronunciation does not establish a dialect. A stylistic pronunciation guide is not a real sociolinguistic survey. Until evidence exists, the unified written reference should not invent lineage, geographic distribution or historical shifts.

## 7. Diachrony and historical etymology

An etymology is a historical claim, not simply a plausible segmentation. `qaroshi` is synchronically analyzable as `qaro + shi`, but that alone does not document when the compound arose. Similarly, a modern technological term might be transparently interpretable without evidence of its invention date. Treat word structure and historical origin as separate database fields.

## 8. Accessible communication

The ability to read the original glyphs should not be a prerequisite for using the grammar lessons. Provide native script, Romanization, IPA and English-style sound-out for important teaching examples. Distinguish phonemic correctness from the approximations in a beginner-friendly cue. Preserve readable focus order, keyboard navigation and semantic headings in the offline wiki; a decorative script background should not reduce contrast or accessibility.

## 9. Social meaning without assumptions about identity

Pronouns in standard Qaroshi are not gendered. The choice between inclusive `naru` and exclusive `muru` can be socially consequential, but it does not imply the participants' actual identity or relationship without context. A sentence with `mali` explicitly indicates romantic love; `nashi` cherishing need not. Readers should interpret the lexeme, not impose a cultural assumption from an English approximate translation.

## 10. Responsible claims

The 30,000-entry dictionary is a **normative editorial reference**. Its counts and machine validations are checkable facts about a data set. Natural frequency, dialect distribution, speaker agreement and historical authenticity require separate evidence. The Academy's exercises teach the standard and do not substitute for community fieldwork.

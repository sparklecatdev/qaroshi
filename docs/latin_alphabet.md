# Latin A–Z: letter names and Qaroshi Romanization

The **Latin alphabet** (A–Z) is a writing system used for English and for the Romanized transcription of Qaroshi. The English *names of letters* and the Qaroshi *sounds of written letters* are different things. Both are listed below. Pronouncing **a letter name** does not tell you how that letter sounds in every English word.

## Special Romanized Qaroshi symbols

Qaroshi uses **26 native letters**, but not the same 26 symbols as English A–Z. The Romanized Qaroshi inventory consists of **21 of the standard Latin letters**, the additional letter **ë**, and the four native letters spelled **sh**, **zh**, **kh**, and **ng** in Latin characters. `c`, `f`, `j`, `v`, and `x` are not assigned native Qaroshi letters.

| Qaroshi Romanization | IPA | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| `ë` | /ə/ | Sixth vowel, distinct from e; sometimes stressed |
| `sh` | /ʃ/ | One native letter, not an s followed by h |
| `zh` | /ʒ/ | One native letter, as in English *measure* |
| `kh` | /x/ | One native letter, velar fricative as in Scottish *loch* |
| `ng` | /ŋ/ | One native letter, as in *sing* |

## Important difference: names vs. sounds

- Latin letter **G** is named **jee** /dʒiː/ in English, but its Qaroshi value is **/ɡ/**.
- Latin letter **Q** is named **cue** /kjuː/ in English, but Qaroshi **q** is **/q/**.
- Latin **Y** is named **why** /waɪ/ in English, but Qaroshi **y** is **/j/**.
- The letters **C, F, J, V, X** remain usable when *writing English or foreign proper names in Latin script*, but a native Qaroshi rendering cannot invent corresponding glyphs for them.
- An English word cannot be pronounced reliably by joining its letter names. The spelling tool in the wiki, **spells letters aloud**, not natural English word phonetics.

## English letter-name spelling examples

- `CAT` spelled letter by letter: /siː eɪ tiː/. This is **not** the word *cat* /kæt/.
- `QARO` spelled letter by letter: /kjuː eɪ ɑɹ oʊ/. The Qaroshi word `qaro` has its own pronunciation /ˈqa.ɾo/.
- `ZIP` spelled letter by letter: /ziː aɪ piː/.

For the **natural phonemic pronunciation of Qaroshi words**, use the dictionary IPA. For **American English letter-name spelling**, use this A–Z chart. Natural English word pronunciation requires a separate English pronunciation dictionary.

## Letter notes

- **A**: Unlike English, Qaroshi a is always /a/.
- **C**: Not a Qaroshi Romanization letter. English c may represent /k/ or /s/; choose the intended sound, not a fixed c substitution.
- **E**: English letter name /iː/ differs from Qaroshi e /e/.
- **F**: Qaroshi has no /f/ letter. Foreign words need phonetic adaptation or a retained Latin spelling.
- **G**: Qaroshi g is always /ɡ/, never English soft g /dʒ/.
- **H**: English letter name may also be haitch in some varieties.
- **I**: Qaroshi i is /i/, not /aɪ/.
- **J**: Not used as a Qaroshi letter; Qaroshi y /j/ is the sound in yes, not English j /dʒ/.
- **O**: Qaroshi o /o/ is a steady monophthong, not American English /oʊ/.
- **Q**: Qaroshi q is /q/, a voiceless uvular stop, not English /kw/ or /k/.
- **R**: Qaroshi r is generally tapped /ɾ/, not American /ɹ/.
- **U**: Qaroshi u is /u/, without the /j/ onset of English letter U.
- **V**: Qaroshi has no native /v/ letter; loanword adaptation depends on the sound.
- **W**: English name has three syllables in many accents; Qaroshi w = /w/.
- **X**: Not a single Qaroshi letter. English x often means /ks/ but is context-dependent.
- **Y**: Qaroshi y represents /j/ like the start of yes; it is not a vowel in the native inventory.
- **Z**: American English zee; British/Commonwealth zed /zɛd/.

# Pronunciation, prosody, and recording standards

The six Qaroshi vowels are /a e i o u ə/. Distinctive consonants include `q` /q/, `kh` /x/, `sh` /ʃ/, `zh` /ʒ/, `ng` /ŋ/, `y` /j/, and tapped `r` /ɾ/. Multi-syllable words normally have penultimate stress; `ë` denotes schwa, not a stress mark. More rapid delivery may merge identical adjacent vowels and tap or roll /r/, but spelling remains stable.

Every dictionary entry has a broad **phonemic** IPA transcription and an English-oriented sound-out approximation. Narrow phonetic recordings cannot be invented from the spelling. Written resonance can influence how a statement is delivered, but its particles do not impose a single universal pitch contour.

The wiki's voice button, where supported, reads an **approximate synthesized sound-out** in a browser voice. It is not a recorded native Qaroshi voice and must be labeled accordingly. A 3,000-word authentic sound archive would require consented speakers, a release license, checked microphone settings, stable word IDs, regional variation notes and manual quality assurance. Until those recordings are supplied, that checklist milestone remains outstanding.

Dialect descriptions likewise require coherent observed differences from real communities. Hypothetical “formal recital” or “casual fast speech” styles are not established regional dialects merely because they sound different.

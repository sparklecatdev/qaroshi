# Qaroshi 8.4 · Qaroshi-first learning and repair laboratory

**A new, complete introductory practice module**, not independent speaker research. The material below uses documented earlier structures plus the 11 *narrowly adopted editorial* 8.4 active verb frames. It gives learners Qaroshi-only text **before** revealing English explanations. The shared public source includes the answer key, so administer a truly blind study using separately distributed copies.

## Reading A: place and uncertainty

```qaroshi
sen mi naro-lo nëra.
sen sa naro-gu gura.
hë sa haro he?
sen sa haro he do mi nu sëmi.
```

In a Qaroshi-only session, ask learners to identify the movement, the location, and what the speaker does *not* claim to know. Have them explain the last line by contrasting it with `sen sa nu haro do mi sëmi.` (a different proposition).

## Reading B: learning and feelings

```qaroshi
sen mi qaroshi-m rëmi.
sen mi kërawë.
më mi ti-m nashi.
wen mi ti-m wënoa.
```

Ask which line makes no assertion of successful mastery, which expresses care without asserting romance, and which explicitly conveys longing through the resonance.

## Reading C: things and participants

```qaroshi
sen mi kima-m sharoni.
sen mi mara-tu kima-m shiwari.
sen mi naro-m rëgë.
sen mi shërami.
```

Ask learners to name the required object in each V1 sentence, the recipient of `shiwari`, the subject of `shërami`, and whether any of the first three lines proves that the requested outcome was achieved.

## Contrast D: all versus not all

```qaroshi
sen ngënao qaro goro.
sen nëgëra qaro goro.
sen ngënao qaro nu goro.
```

A listener should distinguish the meaning of each statement and explain why the second does **not** assert that any Nardoragon flies. A speaker must also recognize that the third is stronger than the second in ordinary nonempty domains.

## After the Qaroshi-only attempt: editorial English reading key

- A: I stay at home. They travel toward home. Are they well? I do not know whether they are well. The comparison line instead means I know that they are not well.
- B: I learn Qaroshi. I try. I deeply cherish you. I miss you/long for you. `nashi` does not automatically imply romance, nor does `kërawë` entail success.
- C: I need water. I send water to a friend. I repair the home. I sleep. The repair can be attempted without proving a restored result. `shiwari` describes sending, not confirmed receipt.
- D: Every Nardoragon flies; not every Nardoragon flies; no Nardoragon flies. The second allows zero fliers, while the first and third cannot both hold for a nonempty domain.

## Extending this into an actual language-independent learning system

1. Give an unfamiliar word three **new Qaroshi examples** using established cases and predicates, and a negative contrast using a different root. Record exact stable lexical IDs.
2. Ask readers to paraphrase the meaning **in Qaroshi without English**. If they cannot, allow a shorter combination of independently established clauses instead of inventing new roots.
3. Record alternate readings and disagreements. Do not decide a word means whatever an English gloss suggests.
4. Add definitions to `data/stress84/core_semantic_cards_120.csv` as **proposals** only. The file is regenerated; permanent approved decisions need a separate reviewed source file. Rebuild then retest.
5. Ask consenting independent people to interpret unknown texts, then share the English target only after their first responses. This supports evidence of actual comprehension, not just example memorization.

An automatically generated score is **not** a substitute for steps 2 through 5. The existing [independent review protocol](REVIEW_PROTOCOL.md) provides privacy and consent guidance.

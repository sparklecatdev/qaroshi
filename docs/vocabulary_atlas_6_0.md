# Qaroshi 6.0 · Vocabulary atlas: 30,000 entries

## What the dictionary counts

Qaroshi's headword inventory now contains **30,000 distinct written entries**. Every entry has a unique romanized spelling, part of speech, English core gloss, topical domain, IPA transcription, and English-oriented sound-out. This number includes **transparent compounds**, which are recognized lexical entries but do not represent 30,000 unrelated memorized roots.

The original 18,663 entries remain unchanged. The new layer contains **924 independently formed bases** plus **10,413 compositional modifier–head compounds**. No tense or plural inflections were counted as entries.

These compounds are productive and often technical. Being standardizable and translatable does **not** mean a compound has been observed in naturally occurring speech or is necessarily equally common in all contexts. For ambiguous phrases, prefer the whole definition and the listed components over word-for-word guessing.

## How to read a compound

The head of a Qaroshi modifier–head noun determines its conceptual category: `[modifier root] + [head root]`. Both roots can be opened independently in the wiki. For example, when a head denotes *monitoring*, its preceding root identifies the thing being monitored. The root combination is written as one Qaroshi word, and it receives penultimate lexical stress according to the regular orthography.

The `components` field records the two original words. The `type` field distinguishes **headword** from **compound**. A word's English field is a concise gloss, not a guaranteed complete sentence translation. Syntax and resonance still govern real utterances.

## Using technical terms in conversation

The supplement contains examples using eight clearly labeled instructional sentence templates. These are *controlled learner examples*, not recorded conversations. Sample topics in real text should be introduced with the topical `-së` suffix and a full sentence-initial resonance marker, e.g.:

`hë qaroshi-së ti shiri-ki he?` • Can you speak about Qaroshi?

`sen qaroshi-së mi shiroli-yu.` • I will write about Qaroshi.

`hë qaroshi-së mi sharoa-ho he?` • May I ask about Qaroshi?

These examples demonstrate case, tense, and modality without requiring every specialized noun to appear in a fabricated real-life story.

## Eighty semantic fields

The expansion uses 80 deliberately bounded concept families spanning natural history, Nardoragon flight culture, physics, mathematics, ecology, medicine, accessibility, law, personal relationships, computing, design, education, agriculture, creative arts, banking, games, and public administration. Each field has independently meaningful base concepts and selected relevant modifier–head combinations.

Use the wiki **Browse topics** section to inspect an entire field at once. Use English search to find a concept's standard rendering, the word page to review its origin, and native-script mode to see the 26 original glyphs.

## Translation guidance and limitations

1. Start with the intended English sense, not a word-for-word string of English tokens.
2. Choose the Qaroshi lemma listed for that sense; consult the part of speech.
3. Use case, aspect, and modality rules from the existing 70-chapter grammar for complete sentences.
4. Choose exactly one sentence-initial written resonance marker from the established 16.
5. Read the IPA as a **phonemic guide**, not a narrow pronunciation recording.
6. For highly specialized terms, describe or paraphrase the concept when speakers may not recognize a rare compound.
7. Avoid translating English plurals, tense endings, and articles as additional dictionary headwords.

## Future lexical work

A 30,000-entry dictionary is not equivalent to natural-language parity. Remaining priorities include polysemy and valency for individual verbs, authentic dialogue and corpus frequency, idioms and figurative senses, additional definitions beyond English translation glosses, speaker feedback, evolving dialect usage, and a usage-ranked everyday core.

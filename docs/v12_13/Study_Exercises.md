# Qaroshi 12–13: Context and Meaning Exercises

The exercises below are tied to the new editorial corpus. They are *not* a listening test or a credential; learners should compare their analyses to the canonical grammar and source text.

## Unit 1: Choosing what was actually said

Read the story **The Map That Wasn't a Map** without English. What did the compass resolve, and what did it leave unanswered? Identify each sentence that states a direct observation and each sentence that relies on an inference or other person's message. Rewrite an unsupported confident prediction as an explicitly uncertain one using `zher` or `ye`. Do not let the resonances override tense or evidence.

**Answer guide:** The compass is a physical instrument, not a guarantee that another participant is permitted or willing to fly. `sen mi wëru-m tika-te si.` explicitly reports a witnessed forest observation, while `sen sa shiwaro-m shiwari-te.` reports a message sent by another person. Confidence about the route is distinct from consent to travel.

## Unit 2: A friend is not a home

Compare `mara`, `naro`, and `runa` using the editorial profile table. Describe a situation where two may apply and the third does not. Find their use in **Two Meanings of Home**. What would be lost by translating all three as “home”?

**Answer guide:** A friend is an interpersonal role, a home is a dwelling or safe place, and chosen family is a trusted social community. Any relationship among the three depends on context rather than being inherent in the dictionary forms.

## Unit 3: Exact limits of a commitment

Read **What a Promise Can Contain**, then write three Qaroshi sentences about the same future plan using `-ki`, `-ho`, and `-du`. Explain how the statements differ and which would be inappropriate as a promise without evidence.

**Answer guide:** `sen mi goro-ki.` communicates capacity, `sen mi goro-ho.` communicates permission, and `qor mi goro-du.` communicates obligation. None alone promises that a flight actually will occur. The future marker `-yu` and the social stance `qor` still require a real referent and realistic conditions.

## Unit 4: Literal and figurative meaning

Read **The river without an answer** in the new anthology, then inspect **A river with no answer** in the metaphor catalogue. Compare the literal river and the proposed interpretation “asking a question of something unable to answer.” Give a new example of a sentence about a river that should remain literal.

**Answer guide:** A river is an inanimate watercourse and cannot respond to an ordinary inquiry. `sen mi kimaro-m tika-te.` is an ordinary physical observation. The figurative interpretation is an editorial proposal, not an established saying that appears whenever a river is mentioned.

## Unit 5: Review a candidate word sense

Open the 5,000-item sense review. Select one verb with the flag “argument structure/valency not individually demonstrated.” Create a sentence with a documented agent, and (if the verb permits it) an overt `-m` object. State why the verb's English gloss alone does not establish all grammatical frames.

**Answer guide:** A bare-subject sentence cannot by itself tell a learner whether the verb is transitive, requires a recipient or permits an oblique. `pëli` can be exemplified with a `-tu` recipient and a `-m` direct object; such marking must be justified by the verb's established frame rather than an English verb's surface pattern.

## Unit 6: Source-of-evidence contrast

A message asserts that a person flew. Write a sentence using `hu`; then write what would be appropriate if the speaker actually witnessed the flight (`si`). Finish with a sentence whose claim is only inferred (`ye`). Avoid treating any of these as proof of truth.

**Answer guide:** `sen sa goro-te hu.` / `sen sa goro-te si.` / `zher sa goro-te ye.` all have the same basic flight proposition but differ in asserted source of information and interpersonal stance. Other resonances can be used when the social intention calls for them.

## Unit 7: Boundaries in conversation

Read **The Softest Refusal** in the new metaphor catalogue and **A friend at the doorway** in the new dialogue set. Explain why a warm resonance does not imply agreement or authorization. What distinction is conveyed by a question ending `he` versus a request ending `ya`?

**Answer guide:** A boundary expressed in a tender tone remains a boundary. `-ho` encodes permission and should not be replaced by `-ki` ability. `he` marks an interrogative clause, while `ya` marks an imperative/request in the documented grammar. Punctuation does not substitute for the appropriate particle.

## Unit 8: Build a literary reading

Choose one of the 18 new texts. Write six connected sentences describing a separate scene with a different outcome. For every sentence, show original Qaroshi, IPA, natural English and a source note. Identify at least one pronoun whose antecedent could be ambiguous and replace it with a clearer noun phrase.

**Review criteria:** The six sentences should share a coherent setting, change the narrative situation at least twice, preserve the resonance and suffix-order rules, and avoid unsupported certainty. A separate human reader should verify that the English translation and contextual meaning agree. A correct grammar parser result is necessary but not sufficient for idiomatic fluency.
